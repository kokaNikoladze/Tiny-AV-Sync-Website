Tiny AV Sync

Formats & limitations

Updated 24 September 2026. Build 10 and the revision below behave differently. Check the build number in About Tiny AV Sync.

Current beta: build 10

Every matched recording becomes a separate 32-bit floating-point PCM track in a MOV. Each track contains silence before and after its recording to fill the video’s duration.

For the same duration, channels and sample rate, 16-bit PCM becomes twice as much audio data; 24-bit becomes one third more. A short recording matched to a long video grows further because of the stored silence. Compressed audio can grow substantially. These comparisons concern audio data, not the entire movie.

Build 10 also converts 32-bit integer input through float32, which does not retain every integer bit. 32-bit integer and 32-bit float are different formats.

Tested revision — not yet released

The revised export policy is per recording. A movie can contain a 24-bit track and a 32-bit float track together, at their respective sample rates. The table describes the candidate, not build 10.

RecordingMatched audio output
16-bit integer PCM16-bit PCM
24-bit integer PCM24-bit PCM
32-bit float PCM32-bit float PCM, including headroom above full scale
FLAC / ALACUncompressed PCM at the identified 16- or 24-bit precision; 20-bit uses 24-bit

32-bit integer, 64-bit float or unidentified lossless precisionRejected with an explanation, rather than silently reduced

Resampling changes sample values. Integer output is dithered after drift correction; exact digital silence stays silent. If the result would exceed the integer range, that recording uses 32-bit float instead. The report and Details show this exception. The app does not normalize or deliberately clip audio.

The candidate stores only each recording’s overlap and positions it on the movie timeline. It does not store silence for the entire video. Automated checks passed for stored timing and native playback decoding. During playback, macOS may generate silence before a recording starts; that silence is not stored as PCM in the movie. Compatibility with individual editing apps still needs checking.

Video, sound and metadata

Matched output is MOV. The first video track is copied without re-encoding. If the track cannot be written to MOV, export fails visibly; there is no hidden video conversion.

Matched recorder files become separate audio tracks. Camera audio is replaced. Additional video tracks, timecode tracks, subtitles and other source tracks are omitted. The revision reports these omissions. Different players and editors may select or mix audio tracks differently.

The pipeline retains the recording’s nominal sample rate and channel count. Drift correction resamples the sound; it is not a bit-for-bit audio copy. Tested channels remain separate. Unusual speaker layouts are not fully qualified.

Some container and video-track metadata, including the video orientation transform, are copied where supported. This is not complete metadata preservation. Camera-specific log/HDR information, Dolby Vision, timecode and editing metadata are not guaranteed. Keep your originals for grading and finishing.

Supported inputs and test evidence

Accepted containers: MOV, MP4, M4V for video; WAV, AIFF, CAF, FLAC, M4A, AAC, MP3 for recordings. A filename extension does not guarantee a readable codec, a reliable match or a successful MOV export. Protected media and multi-track standalone recordings are not supported by the revised policy.

Previous native fixtures passed for H.264 in MP4, HEVC in MOV and ProRes 422 HQ in MOV, with identical encoded video payloads. Audio decoding fixtures covered WAV 16/24/32-bit integer and float32, AIFF 24-bit, CAF float32, FLAC 24-bit, AAC and ALAC in M4A, and MP3. A decoding test does not establish preservation of source precision; the new revision explicitly rejects 32-bit integer.

Previous resampling fixtures passed at 44.1, 48, 88.2, 96, 176.4 and 192 kHz, with 1, 2 and 8 channels, silent-channel separation and float headroom. The revised candidate passed all 20 output-policy checks, including mixed 24-bit/float32 tracks at 48/44.1 kHz, compact storage, exact no-drift samples, silent-channel separation, dithering, float fallback and native timeline decoding. End-to-end synthetic and real-control tests, cancellation, filesystem checks and matcher regressions also passed. Rotation, one variable-frame-rate fixture and one 10-bit HEVC static color-tag fixture passed; these do not establish universal editor or HDR compatibility.

Not fully qualified: M4V, raw AAC, HDR/log metadata, Dolby Vision, unusual channel layouts and every variable-frame-rate profile. Camera RAW, MKV, WebM and proprietary camera formats are not advertised as supported. There is no blanket “all formats” promise.

Matching, results and storage

Matching requires clear shared sound in the camera clip and recording. Silence, unrelated or repetitive sound, very short overlaps, edits and changing clock speeds can prevent a reliable match. Short overlaps may be aligned without drift correction. Check the result before editing.

One recording can match several videos. Several overlapping recordings can match one video. Duplicate inputs may produce duplicate tracks or outputs; there is no automatic deduplication.

A recording used for any successful match is considered used. Its portions outside the videos are not exported separately. Unmatched videos and entirely unused recordings are copied without changing their media bytes. Unsupported or failed items are identified in the report; a failed video export is not a completed result.

Originals are never overwritten. Completed results go into a fresh synced folder. The revision’s avsync-report.json includes source and output audio formats, placement, drift, conversions, omissions and errors. Reports contain filenames and stay on your Mac.

Audio output is uncompressed, and several tracks add up. Temporary decoded recordings and a complete output copy also require disk space. The revision checks combined temporary/output requirements, but other applications can consume space during a job. A failed job may leave completed results; a crash may leave cache files.
